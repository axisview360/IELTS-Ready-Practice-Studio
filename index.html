What's new

  • Three difficulty tiers — Band 5-6, Band 6-7, and Band 7-8+ — for Reading, Listening, Writing and Speaking.
  • Question banks, not single quizzes — Reading and Listening each pull from several passages/recordings per tier; hit "New passage" or "New recording" for a different one.
  • Shuffled answer order — multiple-choice options are re-shuffled every time a quiz loads, so the position of the correct answer isn't a pattern you can memorise.
  • Rotating prompts — Writing charts/essay topics and Speaking cue cards also rotate through a pool per tier.

General exam tips

  • Band scores reward task completion as much as accuracy — always answer every question, even a guess.
  • In Reading and Listening, spelling and word limits are graded strictly.
  • In Writing, a clear structure usually scores higher than beautiful sentences with no plan.
  • In Speaking, fluency and self-correction matter more than a big vocabulary.

Listening skills checklist

  • Before you press play, read every question and its word limit — "no more than two words" is graded strictly, and an extra word makes the whole answer wrong.
  • Use those few seconds of silence to predict what kind of word is missing — a name, a date, a number, a place — so your ear knows what to listen for.
  • The words on the page are almost always a paraphrase of what you'll actually hear, not the exact same words, so don't wait for an exact match.
  • Numbers and letters that sound similar (like 13 vs 30, or B vs D) are a classic trap — say them out loud during practice.
  • In multiple choice, resist writing down the first option you hear — recordings often mention every choice, then correct themselves partway through.
  • Spelling and grammar count as much as hearing the right word — a correct answer spelled wrong is still marked wrong.
  • Never leave a blank. An educated guess can still score; an empty box never can.
